## How we kept track

Nothing scientific. One ordinary kitchen, a notepad stuck to the fridge, and a tick every time a tool came out of the drawer. After two years the pattern was obvious: a handful of things carried almost every meal, and the rest were bought for one recipe and never touched again.

## The seven that stayed

Each of these got used at least weekly for two straight years: a heavy chef’s knife, a large wooden board, a pair of tongs, a fine grater, a set of nesting bowls, a silicone spatula, and a digital thermometer. None of them are expensive, and none of them are exciting.

## The ones that went in a drawer and stayed there

Single-purpose gadgets lost every time. Avocado slicers, garlic presses, strawberry hullers, egg separators: all replaced by a knife and a spoon within a month. If a tool does one job that a knife already does, it is going to live in the back of the drawer.

## What to buy first if you are starting from nothing

Buy the knife and the board first, and spend a little more than feels comfortable on both. Everything else on this list can wait for a sale. A sharp knife on a stable board changes cooking more than any appliance under a hundred dollars.

## How to make them last

Hand-wash the knife and dry it immediately, oil the wooden board once a month, and keep the thermometer battery cover shut. That is the entire maintenance routine, and it is the difference between a two-year tool and a ten-year one.
